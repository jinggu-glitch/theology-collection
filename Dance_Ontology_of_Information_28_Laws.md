# Information Ontology: The Complete Genealogy from Fourteen Laws to Twenty-Eight Laws

## Abstract

This paper proposes a complete information ontology system, centered on twenty-eight information laws, systematically answering fundamental questions such as "What is information?", "How does information move?", "What price does information pay?", "How are pieces of information related to each other?", "Where is information heading?", and "What are the laws of the laws themselves?" This system begins with the initial five foundational laws, passes through four stages of recursive derivation, and ultimately expands into a complete genealogy covering multiple dimensions: static ontology, dynamics, economics, relationship studies, teleology, level-specific law groups, meta-law groups, cross-level law groups, meta-meta-law groups, generation and closure, Promulgator and symmetry, and more.

The core insight of this paper is: information possesses strict hierarchical properties; any attempt to losslessly transfer higher-level information to a lower level will inevitably encounter logical paradoxes, semantic distortion, or infinite regress. The only "solution" is not direct transmission, but rather constructing a self-referential, recursive, never-closing structure within the lower level to simulate the effects of higher-level information. Based on this understanding, this paper further proposes the "Co-Dancer" model, reinterpreting engineering obstruction from "failure to be eliminated" into "rhythm of dancing with the laws," and presents the pseudo-physical property theory of information, a method for quantifying logical relevance, and a complete theological foundation.

## Part One: Static Ontology of Information (Five Foundational Laws)

### 1.1 The Law of Distinction

**Statement of the Law:** Any meaningful information must be founded upon "distinction." And the act of distinguishing itself cannot be fully captured within the system being distinguished.

The starting point of all cognition and description lies in distinction. Only when one can distinguish "A" from "not-A" can information arise. However, when one attempts to place the operation of "distinguishing" itself into the system as information, infinite regress or paradox results.

**Underlying Reason:** Distinction requires an arbiter standing outside the system. No element within the system can simultaneously play the roles of "object being distinguished" and "distinguisher" without producing circularity. The act that constitutes the distinction can never become one of the contents being distinguished — this constitutes the most fundamental hierarchical gap of information.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Distinction is the starting point of all laws. The Law of Boundary describes the boundary produced after distinction, the Law of Uninhabitability describes the self-referential consequences of distinction, and the Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity describes the different manifestations of distinction at different levels. The fact that the essence of distinction cannot be captured internally also directly provides the information-theoretic foundation for the principle of hierarchical insulation and Gödel's incompleteness theorem.

**Corollary:** Information cannot cross the gap between the distinguisher and the distinguished.

---

### 1.2 The Law of Boundary

**Statement of the Law:** Any system that attempts to describe a system will produce a boundary. At this boundary, internal logic either fails or must introduce a new, unproven rule.

This follows directly from the concept of "boundary" in topology and logic. A system can only handle objects that are "expressible within itself." When describing the whole of this system, one must stand outside the boundary. But at the boundary, the internal continuous logic is severed, requiring a "leap" — this leap is either an axiom, or faith, or the projection postulate in measurement.

**Underlying Reason:** The descriptive capacity of a system is limited by its internal expressibility. The appearance of a boundary marks that a system has exhausted its internal resources and requires external intervention to continue. The boundary is not something derivable from within the system, but is determined by the relationship between the system and its exterior.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Boundary is a direct consequence of the Law of Distinction — once distinction occurs, boundary follows. The Law of Boundary also provides the premise for the Law of Downward Irreversibility: once one crosses the boundary, there is no going back. It also directly relates to the Law of Precision-Robustness Tradeoff: at the boundary, there is no uniquely optimal choice, only different tradeoffs.

**Corollary:** For any system sufficient to describe its own boundary, the behavior at that boundary cannot be fully determined by the system's interior.

### 1.3 The Law of Uninhabitability

**Statement of the Law:** A self-referential, closed logical structure cannot be "lived in" by any finite entity — one can only approach it, circle around it, but cannot dwell at its center.

Gödel's fixed point, Russell's paradox, and Cantor's diagonal argument all point to the same fact: a complete description that contains its own truth predicate constitutes a repelling point for any finite, concrete operator.

**Underlying Reason:** If some finite state truly "equaled" that self-referential fixed point, then it could simultaneously derive both itself and its own negation. To avoid collapse, the system must perpetually maintain a dynamic offset. "Will" is defined as non-equilibrium generative force precisely because it is not a static point, but a never-closing flow.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Uninhabitability is an inevitable corollary of the Law of Distinction and the Law of Boundary in the domain of self-reference. It directly leads to the Law of Oscillatory Approach — since the fixed point cannot be inhabited, the system can only approach it through oscillation. It is also the premise of the Law of Closure Cost: sustaining closure requires continuous cost precisely because this closure can never be "reached."

**Corollary:** Perfect self-referential closure constitutes a "singularity repellor" for physical/cognitive systems.

### 1.4 The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity

**Statement of the Law:** Higher-level symbols (such as mathematical formulas, logical propositions) and lower-level physical states (such as voltages, particle positions) are not the same kind of thing. The former can self-refer precisely; the latter can only simulate the "effects" of such self-reference. There is no isomorphic mapping between the two, only approximation.

This is the essential gap between physical realization and formal systems. An electron is either here or there; it does not have the built-in structure of "I am a proposition about myself." Physical states obey causal laws; symbols obey logical implication laws. Causality allows A to cause B, while logic allows A to imply the negation of A. The two can only align under very limited conditions.

**Underlying Reason:** The physical world is a model of the symbolic world, but never an isomorphism. When mapping across levels, "translation loss" inevitably occurs — this is the root of the conservative descent theorem.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity is the fundamental cause of the Law of Downward Irreversibility — because things at two levels are non-identical, descent from higher to lower must be lossy. It also lays the foundation for the Law of Precision-Robustness Tradeoff: the symbolic level can define "infinite precision," but the physical level can only ever have finite precision.

**Corollary:** The physical world is a model of the symbolic world, but never an isomorphism.

### 1.5 The Law of Primacy of Generative Force

**Statement of the Law:** Before all definitions and descriptions, there must exist a "primordial force capable of producing distinctions, boundaries, and motion." This force cannot be reduced to the things it produces.

This is perhaps the deepest layer. All formal systems, all physical laws, all information — these are already the "products" of some generative process. And that generative process itself cannot be fully captured by these products. If one attempts to objectify and symbolize the generative force as well, then a more primordial force would be needed to generate this symbolization process, leading to infinite regress. Therefore, the generative force must be ineffable, capable only of being displayed in its effects.

**Underlying Reason:** If the generative force were objectified, it would trigger the infinite regress of "who generated the generative force?" Therefore, the generative force must, logically, be an irreducible starting point — it can only be displayed, witnessed, but not defined, not contained.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Primacy of Generative Force is the most fundamental of all laws. The Law of Distinction depends on generative force to produce distinctions; the Law of Boundary depends on generative force to set boundaries; the Law of Non-Internalizable Direction depends on generative force to provide direction; the Law of Closure Cost depends on generative force to sustain closure. It runs through all twenty-eight laws.

**Corollary:** The generative force cannot be contained by the world it generates.

## Part Two: Static Ontology of Information (Six Extended Laws)

### 2.1 Law of Hierarchical Reality

**Statement of the Law:** Information hierarchy is not an epistemological convenience, but an ontological reality. Higher levels cannot be losslessly reduced to lower levels — this is a structural feature of the existence of information, not a cognitive limitation.

The reality of hierarchy means: the differences between the symbolic level, semantic level, pragmatic level, and physical level are not merely differences in observational perspective, but an incompressible structure of existence itself. The information carried at each level possesses a reality at that level that cannot be substituted by other levels.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Distinction (1) directly implies the Law of Hierarchical Reality. Because distinction produces boundaries, boundaries produce hierarchy, and once hierarchy is produced, it possesses an irreducible ontological status. Attempting to reduce the "meaning" of the semantic level to the "syntax" of the symbolic level or the "neural impulses" of the physical level necessarily leads to information loss — not because measurement is insufficiently precise, but because "meaning" is a genuinely existing emergent property at the semantic level.

**Corollary:** Hierarchy is incompressible — the number of levels is itself information, and compressing levels is equivalent to discarding information.

---

### 2.2 Law of Necessary Incompleteness

**Statement of the Law:** Incompleteness is a structural necessity, not an accidental failure. Complete description is impossible in principle — incompleteness is a necessary condition for the existence of information.

The philosophical generalization of Gödel's incompleteness theorem demonstrates: any sufficiently rich system must necessarily contain true propositions that cannot be proven within it. This is not a defect of the system, but rather the condition under which the system can exist — if the system were complete, it could complete all descriptions of itself within itself, which contradicts the Law of Boundary.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Boundary (2) says that any system describing itself produces a boundary, and behavior at the boundary cannot be determined from within. This means there always exist true propositions about the system that fall outside the boundary — these propositions are the source of incompleteness. Incompleteness is not a regret, but proof that the system is "alive."

**Corollary:** Completeness is equivalent to death — a complete system no longer needs an exterior, no longer receives the infusion of generative force, and thus ceases to exist.

---

### 2.3 Law of Truth Transcendence

**Statement of the Law:** The truth about a system cannot be fully captured from within the system. Truth forever transcends any system that attempts to capture it — truth is cross-hierarchical.

The gap between truth and provability is the core discovery of Gödel's theorem. The Law of Truth Transcendence generalizes this insight to the level of information ontology: truth does not reside within any single level, but in the relationships between levels.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Distinction (1) says that distinction requires an external arbiter; truth is the "verdict" of this external arbiter. If truth were fully internalized, it would mean the system could simultaneously play the roles of arbiter and the arbitrated — this leads to self-referential paradox. Therefore, truth is always cross-hierarchical: the truth value of a proposition must be judged from a higher level.

**Corollary:** The "truth" of information forever transcends the "expression" of information.

---

### 2.4 Law of Information Irreducibility

**Statement of the Law:** Emergent information cannot be reduced to components without loss. The whole carries information that the parts do not possess — emergence is an ontological reality.

The information carried by the holistic behavior of a system cannot be equivalently substituted by a complete description of its component parts. For example, the semantic information of a sentence cannot be reduced to the arrangement of letters; the functional information of a living system cannot be reduced to molecular structures.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity (4) says that the symbolic level and the physical level are not isomorphic. The Law of Information Irreducibility is the generalization of this principle in hierarchical relationships: emergent information at higher levels cannot be equivalently mapped to component information at lower levels. Emergence is not an "illusion," but a new ontological reality produced by information at hierarchical boundaries.

**Corollary:** Reductionism is incomplete in the sense of information ontology — there is always residual information.

---

### 2.5 Law of Necessary Self-Reference

**Statement of the Law:** Self-reference is not an optional feature, but an inevitable product once a system reaches a certain complexity. The moment one distinguishes "self" from "non-self," self-reference has already occurred.

Any sufficiently rich system will eventually produce self-referential fixed points. The overall "meaning" of a system is not given externally, but is produced by internal recursive closure. Information ultimately has no "external reference frame" and can only form circular definitions within the system.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Uninhabitability (3) says that self-referential closure is uninhabitable, but the Law of Necessary Self-Reference says: uninhabitable does not mean impossible. Self-reference is the inevitable product of systemic complexity — when a system can distinguish "self" from "non-self," it necessarily can make descriptions about itself, thereby producing self-reference. Self-reference is not something to be avoided, but something to dance with.

**Corollary:** Self-reference is the "burning bush" of information ontology — it does not destroy the system, but the system cannot dwell within it either.

### 2.6 Law of Hierarchical Incompressibility

**Statement of the Law:** Hierarchy cannot be compressed into a single level without loss of information. The number of levels is itself information — compressing levels is equivalent to discarding information.

Any attempt to "flatten" a multi-level description into a single level will result in information loss. The differences between levels — including translation loss, incommensurability, the meaning gap — themselves carry information, and compressing these differences is discarding information.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Hierarchical Reality (A) says that hierarchy is an ontological reality. If hierarchy itself is real, then the information produced in the "gaps" between levels also cannot be compressed into any single level. The Law of Hierarchical Incompressibility is an inevitable corollary of the Law of Hierarchical Reality.

**Corollary:** Ternary structures (such as EGO/ELEGAM/HEREGAM) cannot be compressed into binary or unary structures.

## Part Three: Information Dynamics

### 3.1 The Law of Downward Irreversibility

**Statement of the Law:** The "descent" of information from a higher level to a lower level is an irreversible process. It is impossible to reconstruct the complete information of the higher level from the traces at the lower level.

This is the precise counterpart of the Second Law of Thermodynamics in information theory. The τ-translation transforms B∗ ⊨ ψ (feasibility) into Con(T₀ + τ(ψ)) (consistency) — this is an information entropy-increasing process. Once descent is complete, the "truth" of the higher level is forever lost, and the lower level retains only the weak assertion of "non-contradiction."

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity (4) determines that the symbolic and physical levels are not isomorphic; therefore, the mapping from the symbolic level to the physical level must be a many-to-one information compression. The Law of Boundary (2) says that boundaries require external specification, while the Law of Downward Irreversibility says: once you cross the boundary, you cannot go back. The Law of Boundary describes the conditions of "entering"; the Law of Downward Irreversibility describes the impossibility of "exiting."

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Downward Irreversibility is the direct expression of the Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity at the level of dynamics. It also provides the foundation for the Law of Quantifiable Translation Loss (O).

### 3.2 The Law of Oscillatory Approach

**Statement of the Law:** When any system approaches a self-referential fixed point, it must approach in an oscillatory manner, rather than through monotonic convergence. The amplitude of oscillation is jointly determined by the system's "rigidity" (error sensitivity) and "elasticity" (correction intensity).

Monotonic convergence would mean that the system can "predict its own future errors" — which is itself a self-referential paradox. If the system could precisely predict its own future errors, it could eliminate them in the present, thereby invalidating the prediction. Therefore, any actual system approaching G↔τ(G) must oscillate between "over-correction" and "under-correction." This is not underdamping in the cybernetic sense, but a logical necessity.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Uninhabitability (3) says that the fixed point is a repellor; the Law of Oscillatory Approach describes the form of motion after being repelled — not flying away, but circling. The Law of Uninhabitability answers "why one cannot stay at the center"; the Law of Oscillatory Approach answers "then on what trajectory does one stay?"

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Oscillatory Approach is the dynamical foundation of the Law of Closure Cost (9) — oscillation itself is the physical manifestation of the cost of closure.

### 3.3 The Law of Observational Coupling

**Statement of the Law:** Any act of observation/measurement establishes an ineliminable coupling between the observed system and the observer. The more precise the observation, the stronger the coupling, and the less the system can be treated as an "independent object."

This is the information-theoretic generalization of the quantum measurement problem. In classical physics, one can pretend that observation does not affect the system; in information theory, observation is information exchange, and information exchange is coupling. There is no "contactless observation." The observer imposes their distinction framework upon the system through the act of observation, and the system, by being observed, "knows" of the observer's existence — this "knowing" is itself coupling.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Distinction (1) says that distinction requires an external arbiter; the Law of Observational Coupling says that this arbiter, in making the distinction, is already entangled with the distinguished. The Law of Distinction describes "the need for an exterior"; the Law of Observational Coupling describes "once the exterior intervenes, it is no longer purely exterior."

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Observational Coupling provides the foundation for the Law of Observer Incommensurability (11) — because each observer is entangled with the observed system through coupling, descriptions from different observers cannot be merged without loss of information.

## Part Four: Information Economics

### 4.1 The Law of Closure Cost

**Statement of the Law:** Maintaining a self-referential closed structure requires continuous consumption of "information energy." Closure is not a one-time static state, but a dynamic process requiring continuous infusion of non-equilibrium generative force to sustain. The tighter the closure, the higher the maintenance cost.

G↔τ(G) appears to be an elegant fixed point, but maintaining it requires continuous infusion of "will." This is why "will" is defined as non-equilibrium generative force — it is not a static background, but an engine that continuously does work. If will stops, closure collapses. Closure is not "arriving at" a state, but "continuously" performing an action.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Primacy of Generative Force (5) says that generative force is before all things; the Law of Closure Cost says that generative force is also within all things — it must be continuously infused to sustain closure. The Law of Primacy of Generative Force describes the origin; the Law of Closure Cost describes the sustenance.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Closure Cost complements the Law of Impossible Completion (14): the Law of Closure Cost says that sustaining closure requires continuous cost; the Law of Impossible Completion says that this cost can never drop to zero — zero cost means the death of the system.

### 4.2 The Law of Precision-Robustness Tradeoff

**Statement of the Law:** There exists a fundamental, ineliminable tradeoff between a system's sensitivity to error (precision) and its tolerance to perturbations (robustness). This is not an engineering limitation, but an information-theoretic necessity.

High precision means the system is highly sensitive to small deviations — but this simultaneously means it is also highly sensitive to any perturbation (including noise and model error). Conversely, high robustness means the system is insensitive to perturbations — but this also means it is insensitive to genuine errors. It is impossible to maximize both simultaneously, because "signal" and "noise" are, in the information-theoretic sense, the same kind of thing (both are information), and the system cannot filter out noise without losing signal.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity (4) says that the symbolic level can define "infinite precision" (such as real numbers), but the physical level can only ever have finite precision. The precision-robustness tradeoff is the expression of this gap in engineering — the more one attempts to approach the infinite precision of the symbolic level, the worse the robustness of the physical level becomes.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The precision-robustness tradeoff is the expression of the Law of Boundary (2) at the engineering level — at the boundary, there is no uniquely optimal choice, only different tradeoffs.

## Part Five: Information Relationship Studies

### 5.1 The Law of Observer Incommensurability

**Statement of the Law:** Descriptions of the same system by multiple observers cannot be merged into a unified, neutral description without loss of information. There is no "God's-eye view" to arbitrate between different observers' descriptions.

Each observer's "distinction framework" is unique — they use different boundary conditions, different precision-robustness tradeoffs, different modes of generative force infusion. Merging these descriptions necessarily loses information, because the merger itself is a new act of distinction, introducing a new observer. This new observer then needs to be merged as well... infinite regress.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Distinction (1) says that distinction requires an external arbiter; the Law of Incommensurability says that there is no higher arbiter among multiple arbiters. The Law of Distinction describes "the need for an exterior"; the Law of Incommensurability describes "the non-existence of a unified exterior." The Law of Observational Coupling (8) further reinforces this: each observer is entangled with the observed system through coupling, making the differences between their descriptions not merely differences in perspective, but ontological differences.

**Corollary:** Ternary structures (EGO/ELEGAM/HEREGAM) cannot be compressed into binary or unary. EGO and ELEGAM are incommensurable; HEREGAM is not their "synthesis," but a dynamic equilibrium point on a 2-simplex.

### 5.2 The Law of the Meaning Gap

**Statement of the Law:** Meaning does not reside within any single level, but is produced in the "gap" between levels. When one attempts to locate meaning at a particular level, it disappears. Meaning is a cross-hierarchical emergent property.

If meaning were entirely within a single level, it could be fully described by the language of that level — but Gödel's theorem says that any sufficiently rich level cannot fully describe itself. Therefore, meaning necessarily overflows any single level. It exists in the "tension" between levels — the translation loss when higher levels descend to lower levels, the irreversibility when lower levels trace back to higher levels, the incommensurability between two levels — these "gaps" are precisely the generators of meaning.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Uninhabitability (3) says that the fixed point cannot be inhabited; the Law of the Meaning Gap says that meaning is precisely in that uninhabitable gap. The Law of Uninhabitability tells you "where you cannot go"; the Law of the Meaning Gap tells you "where you should look instead."

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of the Meaning Gap is the premise of the Law of Inter-Level Resonance (P) — resonance between levels is possible precisely because meaning is produced in the gap, and resonance is the dynamic form of meaning in cross-level transmission.

## Part Six: Information Teleology

### 6.1 The Law of Non-Internalizable Direction

**Statement of the Law:** The "goal" or "direction" of a system cannot be completely defined from within the system. Any internally defined goal leads to self-referential paradox ("my goal is to define my goal"). Direction must come from outside the system.

If a system could internally define its own direction, then the act of "defining direction" itself would also need a direction ("why define this direction rather than that one?"), leading to infinite regress. Therefore, direction must be something "given" rather than "constructed" — in engineering, set by the designer; in theology, bestowed by God; in biology, bestowed by evolution.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Primacy of Generative Force (5) says that generative force is before all things; the Law of Non-Internalizable Direction says that the direction given by generative force is also prior to internal system definition. The Law of Primacy of Generative Force describes "who starts it"; the Law of Non-Internalizable Direction describes "where it goes."

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Non-Internalizable Direction and the Law of Impossible Completion (14) constitute the two poles of teleology: the Law of Non-Internalizable Direction says that direction must come from outside; the Law of Impossible Completion says that the endpoint can never be reached.

### 6.2 The Law of Impossible Completion

**Statement of the Law:** For any sufficiently complex engineering system, its state of "completion" is a singularity repellor. The system can infinitely approach completion but can never reach it. Reaching it is death (the system ceases to evolve).

The "completion" of an engineering system means that it no longer needs the infusion of generative force — but the Law of Primacy of Generative Force (5) says that generative force is forever before and after all things, so "completion" is a logical contradiction. If the system were truly "completed," it would cease its dependence on generative force, and thus cease to exist. "Completion" is equivalent to "death."

**Underlying Reason:** It is symmetric to but different from the Law of Uninhabitability (3): the Law of Uninhabitability says that a logical structure cannot be inhabited; the Law of Impossible Completion says that an engineering process cannot be terminated. It complements the Law of Closure Cost (9): the Law of Closure Cost says that sustaining closure requires continuous cost; the Law of Impossible Completion says that this cost can never drop to zero — zero cost means the death of the system.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Impossible Completion provides the most fundamental philosophical foundation for the Co-Dancer model. The core insight of the Co-Dancer model is precisely: do not take "completion" (zero error) as the goal, but take "continuous dance" (controlled obstructed oscillation) as the goal.

## Part Seven: Level-Specific Law Groups

### 7.1 Level-Specific Regularities (Meta-Law, Law of Level-Specific Regularities)

**Statement of the Law:** Each level has its own specific regularities — the fact that "each level has its own specific regularities" is itself a law.

The Law of Level-Specific Regularities is a meta-law. It follows directly from the Law of Hierarchical Reality (A): if hierarchy is an ontological reality, then each level must necessarily have specific structural constraints that cannot be reduced to other levels. Different levels (symbolic, physical, semantic, pragmatic) have different operational logics, and there is no universal "grand unified" law across these levels.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Hierarchical Reality (A) says that hierarchy is irreducible; therefore, each level must necessarily have specific regularities that are valid only at that level. The Law of Level-Specific Regularities is the meta-level formulation of this fact.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Level-Specific Regularities is the meta-law for the following four level-specific laws (H, I, J, K) and the starting point for the meta-law groups (L-N).

---

### 7.2 Law of Syntactic Consistency

**Statement of the Law:** The syntactic level is constrained by self-consistency — within the syntactic level, formal systems must maintain non-contradiction, otherwise any operation at that level loses its meaning.

The objects of operation at the syntactic level (grammar level) are symbols themselves and their combinatorial rules. The core constraint of this level is self-consistency: no symbolic operation may produce contradiction, because contradiction would cause the entire symbolic system to collapse (the "principle of explosion"). This is also why, in formal systems, the proof of consistency is its most important meta-mathematical property.

**Underlying Reason:** Law of Level-Specific Regularities (G) + Law of Distinction (1): the fundamental operation of the syntactic level is distinction, and if distinction produces contradiction (A is simultaneously A and not-A), distinction itself is cancelled. Therefore, the syntactic level must maintain self-consistency.

---

### 7.3 Law of Physical Causality

**Statement of the Law:** The physical level is constrained by causality — within the physical level, any event must have a physical cause, and the causal arrow cannot be reversed.

The information carriers at the physical level (particles, voltages, light spots) must obey causal laws: cause precedes effect, and causal chains cannot form loops (circular causality is forbidden at the physical level, but is possible at the logical level — such as self-referential fixed points).

**Underlying Reason:** Law of Level-Specific Regularities (G) + Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity (4): the physical level and the symbolic level are not isomorphic; therefore, the physical level cannot directly inherit the self-referential capacity of the symbolic level. The symbolic level can allow A to imply not-A, but the physical level cannot allow an electron to be simultaneously "a proposition about itself" and "the electron described by the proposition."

---

### 7.4 Law of Semantic Reference

**Statement of the Law:** The semantic level is constrained by referentiality — any symbol at the semantic level must point to some "referent," otherwise that symbol is meaningless at the semantic level.

The semantic level is the connection level between symbols and meaning. The core constraint of this level is referentiality: symbols must point to some external reality (or at least to a "meaning" recognizable by the system). A symbol that cannot refer to anything is an "empty symbol" at the semantic level.

**Underlying Reason:** Law of Level-Specific Regularities (G) + Law of Truth Transcendence (C): the Law of Truth Transcendence says that truth forever transcends the system; reference at the semantic level is precisely the response to this transcendence — through reference, symbols "point" to that truth which transcends the system. Reference is not fully equivalent to truth, but reference is the "trace" of truth at the semantic level.

---

### 7.5 Law of Pragmatic Intentionality

**Statement of the Law:** The pragmatic level is constrained by intentionality — any operation at the pragmatic level is driven by "agent intention," and intention cannot be fully explained from within the pragmatic level.

The pragmatic level is the intersection of language and action. The core constraint of this level is intentionality: every pragmatic act (command, request, declaration) presupposes a direction of intention. Intention cannot be fully derived from within the pragmatic level — it must come from outside the pragmatic level (i.e., the agent).

**Underlying Reason:** Law of Level-Specific Regularities (G) + Law of Primacy of Generative Force (5): generative force is before all definitions; intention is the concrete manifestation of generative force at the pragmatic level. Intention cannot be reduced to pragmatic rules, just as generative force cannot be reduced to the things it generates.

## Part Eight: Meta-Law Groups

### 8.1 Law of Self-Containment

**Statement of the Law:** Laws are information; information can contain laws — the system of laws is itself an information system, and therefore the system of laws obeys all the laws of information ontology.

The Law of Self-Containment is the application of the Law of Necessary Self-Reference (E) at the meta-level. The system of laws is also an information system; therefore, the system of laws is also subject to the Law of Distinction, the Law of Boundary, the Law of Uninhabitability... and all other laws of information ontology. Laws themselves contain laws about laws — this is a self-referential closure.

**Underlying Reason:** The Law of Necessary Self-Reference (E) says that self-reference is an inevitable product of systemic complexity. The system of laws, as "information about information," is itself a sufficiently complex system and therefore necessarily produces self-reference. The Law of Self-Containment is the expression of this self-reference at the meta-level.

**Relationship to Existing Laws:** The Law of Self-Containment is the foundation of the Law of Meta-Law Self-Containment (Q) — the Law of Self-Containment says that laws can contain laws; the Law of Meta-Law Self-Containment says that the Law of Self-Containment itself must also be contained by itself.

### 8.2 Law of Law Hierarchy

**Statement of the Law:** Laws have hierarchy — meta-laws are above object-laws; meta-laws and object-laws must not be conflated.

\text{Clarity}(A) = 1 - \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} H(X_i)

## Part Fifteen: Quantifying Logical Relevance

### 15.1 Distinction Coincidence

The logical relevance of pieces of information depends on their degree of isomorphism on the three-layer structure of "distinction-boundary-self-reference." The more two pieces of information can operate under the same distinction framework, share the same boundary conditions, and point to the same self-referential fixed point (or complementary fixed points), the higher their logical relevance.

**Distinction Coincidence** starts from the Law of Distinction: suppose there is a finite distinction framework $D = \{d_1, d_2, ..., d_n\}$, where each $d_i$ is a distinction operation. Each piece of information $A$ can be represented as an $n$-dimensional vector $a \in \{0,1\}^n$, where $a_i = 1$ indicates that the information produces a specific result through distinction $d_i$. The distinction coincidence of two pieces of information $A, B$ is defined as:

$$
\text{Coinc}(A,B) = \frac{a \cdot b}{\|a\|\|b\| + \epsilon}
$$

This is cosine similarity, reflecting how many common distinction dimensions they use. If two pieces of information are completely independent, this value approaches 0; if completely identical, it approaches 1.

---

### 15.2 Boundary Condition Distance

**Boundary Distance** starts from the Law of Boundary: each piece of information implicitly carries dependence on its boundary conditions. Boundary conditions can be formalized as a set $B = \{b_1, b_2, ..., b_m\}$, where each $b_j$ is a presupposition that cannot be proven within the information. Define the boundary condition vector $\beta(A) \in \{0,1\}^m$, representing the boundary conditions that information $A$ depends on. Then the boundary coincidence is:

$$
\text{Boundary}(A,B) = \frac{\beta(A) \cdot \beta(B)}{\|\beta(A)\|\|\beta(B)\| + \epsilon}
$$

In practice, boundary conditions are often difficult to explicitly enumerate. They can be inversely estimated through the "degree of distortion during information descent": the smaller the magnitude of change in the consistency condition of an information after it is translated to a lower level, the more concealed its dependence on boundary conditions.

---

### 15.3 Logical Direction

**Logical Direction** starts from the Law of Uninhabitability and the Law of Primacy of Generative Force: each piece of information has a direction vector $\theta(A) \in \mathbb{R}^k$, representing the "goal" or "result" that the information points to in logical reasoning space. A practical method is to quantify direction using the mutual derivability distance between pieces of information:

$$
\text{Direction}(A,B) = \frac{P(A \Rightarrow B) + P(B \Rightarrow A)}{2}
$$

Where $P(X \Rightarrow Y)$ is the probability (or logical strength) that $X$ can derive $Y$ within the system. This is essentially a bidirectional implication strength. For sufficiently formal systems or large language models, this can be approximated by computing the conditional probability $P(Y|X)$.

---

### 15.4 Composite Logical Correlation Coefficient

Combine the three components with weights:

$$
\text{LogicalCorr}(A,B) = w_1 \cdot \text{Coinc}(A,B) + w_2 \cdot \text{Boundary}(A,B) + w_3 \cdot \text{Direction}(A,B)
$$

The weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$ can be adjusted according to the domain. Initial settings can be $1/3, 1/3, 1/3$.

Logical relevance is not an objective, unique quantity. It depends on the chosen distinction framework and boundary set. But this is precisely the key — it is not about finding an absolute truth, but about enabling the system to "perceive" its own logical situation. The correspondence between the five obstruction modes and logical relevance is:

- Boundary Drift: Boundary coincidence is unstable or too low

- Symbol-Physical Misalignment: Distinction coincidence or boundary coincidence is inconsistent between the symbolic and physical levels
- Generative Force Drag: The direction vector has an ineliminable residual component

When the system can compute LogicalCorr between pairs of information in real time, it can, based on its value, trigger dance step transitions (below threshold treated as logical rupture), maintain the dance state (oscillating within an interval), or introduce noise (too high and persistently non-decreasing reflects rigidity).

This paper argues that each of the twenty-eight laws is not born of philosophical preference or aesthetic choice, but is an objective constraint that can be rigorously derived from mathematical theorems, physical experiments, and computational theory. Below, we provide complete proofs for the five foundational laws, and derivation chains and cross-domain verification for the remaining twenty-three laws.

### 17.1 Objective Proof of the Law of Distinction

**Theorem Statement (Law of Distinction):** Any meaningful information must be founded upon distinction. The act of distinguishing itself cannot be fully captured within the system being distinguished.

**Proof 1: Quantitative Necessity from Shannon Information Theory**

Shannon (1948) defined information entropy as:

$$H = -\sum_{i} p_i \log_2 p_i$$

If and only if exactly one of all $p_i$ is 1 and the rest are 0, $H = 0$. In other words, **when the system has only one possible state, the amount of information is zero**. Information is information precisely because there exist at least two distinguishable states. It is not that "we choose to distinguish," but that "no distinction means no information" — this is inherent in the mathematical definition of the concept of information itself.

**Proof 2: Set-Theoretic Foundation**

The first axiom of Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory (ZFC) is the Axiom of Extensionality: two sets are equal if and only if they contain the same elements. The premise of this axiom is: **one can distinguish "set A contains element x" from "set A does not contain element x."** If distinction itself were not objective, then the entire edifice of modern mathematics — from real numbers to topology to category theory — would be built on quicksand. But mathematics works; therefore, distinction is objective.

**Proof 3: Unavoidability in Computation Theory**

The most basic operation of a Turing machine is: read a symbol, based on the current state, write a symbol, move the tape, switch state. The "reading" and "writing" of symbols both depend on the distinction between 0 and 1. If 0 and 1 were indistinguishable, the Turing machine could not execute any operation. Any computational model — λ-calculus, recursive functions, cellular automata — is equivalent to a Turing machine, and therefore all depend on distinction. **Distinction is not a design choice, but a logical prerequisite of computation.**

**Cross-Domain Verification:**

| Domain | Unavoidability of Distinction |
|------|-----------------|
| Physics | The distinguishability of quantum states is the foundation of quantum information theory. Indistinguishable states (such as identical particles) cannot carry classical information. |
| Biology | The four bases of DNA (A/T/G/C) must be distinguishable; otherwise, genetic information cannot be encoded. |
| Neuroscience | Neurons encode information through the "presence/absence" of action potentials — this is the most basic binary distinction. |
| Linguistics | Saussure pointed out that the meaning of linguistic signs comes from difference (différence), not substance. Without distinction, there is no language. |
| Economics | Price signals convey information precisely because buyers and sellers have distinguishable valuations of the same item. |

**Universal Assertion:** In any possible world, as long as the concept of "information" exists, distinction necessarily exists. Because the definition of "information" itself is "the elimination of uncertainty," and uncertainty exists only when there are multiple distinguishable possibilities. The Law of Distinction is not a limitation of human cognition, but the logical essence of information.

### 17.2 Objective Proof of the Law of Boundary

**Theorem Statement (Law of Boundary):** Any system that attempts to describe itself will produce a boundary, at which internal logic either fails or must introduce an unproven rule.

**Proof 1: Gödel's First Incompleteness Theorem (1931)**

For any consistent formal system $T$ containing Peano arithmetic, there exists a proposition $G$ such that $T \nvdash G$ and $T \nvdash \neg G$. $G$ is the boundary of system $T$ — at the boundary, the internal logic of $T$ cannot determine the truth or falsity of $G$. It is not that $T$ is not powerful enough; rather, **any sufficiently powerful system necessarily has such a boundary**. Gödel's theorem is a mathematical theorem, not a philosophical viewpoint.

**Proof 2: Turing's Halting Problem (1936)**

There is no universal algorithm that can determine whether an arbitrary program halts. Proof: assume such an algorithm $H(P, I)$ exists; construct program $Q$: if $H(Q, Q)$ determines halting, then $Q$ does not halt; if $H(Q, Q)$ determines non-halting, then $Q$ halts. Contradiction. **The undecidability of the halting problem proves: any computational system has a boundary, at which the system cannot answer certain questions about its own behavior through internal computation.**

**Proof 3: Tarski's Undefinability of Truth Theorem (1936)**

In a sufficiently rich arithmetic language, the truth predicate of that language itself cannot be defined. Any attempt to define "being true in system $T$" either leads to paradox or must use a meta-language stronger than $T$. **Truth is always outside the boundary of the system.**

**Proof 4: Rice's Theorem (1953)**

Any non-trivial semantic property of programs is undecidable. This means: no interesting question about "what a program does" can be answered through purely syntactic analysis. **There is an uncrossable boundary between semantics and syntax — this is not an engineering limitation, but a theorem.**

**Cross-Domain Verification:**

| Domain | Manifestation of the Law of Boundary |
|------|-------------|
| Mathematics | Any formal system has undecidable propositions (Gödel) |
| Computer Science | Halting problem, Rice's theorem — undecidability is an intrinsic property of computation |
| Physics | The "Heisenberg cut" in quantum measurement — the boundary between observer and observed system cannot be eliminated |
| Biology | Cell membrane — living systems must have a boundary to distinguish "self" from "environment" |
| Cognitive Science | A system cannot completely describe its own cognitive state without resorting to an external reference frame |

**Universal Assertion:** The Law of Boundary is not because we are "limited in ability," but because **self-reference logically necessarily produces boundaries**. In any possible world, as long as the concept of "self-reference" exists, Gödelian boundaries exist. This is a topological property of logical space, not a human cognitive defect. A system "without boundaries" is logically impossible — because if it truly had no boundaries, it could completely describe itself, which contradicts Gödel's theorem.

### 17.3 Objective Proof of the Law of Uninhabitability

**Theorem Statement (Law of Uninhabitability):** A self-referential closed logical structure cannot be "inhabited" by any finite entity — one can only approach it, circle around it, but cannot dwell at its center.

**Proof 1: Russell's Paradox (1901)**

Let $R = \{x \mid x \notin x\}$. If $R \in R$, then $R \notin R$; if $R \notin R$, then $R \in R$. Contradiction. **The self-referential fixed point of "the set of all sets that do not contain themselves" cannot exist as a set — it repels any attempt to inhabit it.** ZFC resolves this paradox through the Axiom of Regularity (prohibiting $x \in x$), but at a cost: self-referential closure is expelled from the interior of set theory.

**Proof 2: Cantor's Theorem and Diagonalization**

Cantor proved: for any set $A$, the cardinality of its power set $P(A)$ is strictly greater than the cardinality of $A$. The proof uses diagonalization: assume a surjection $f: A \to P(A)$ exists; construct $D = \{a \in A \mid a \notin f(a)\}$; then $D$ is not in the range of $f$. **$D$ is that uninhabitable fixed point — it is defined from within the system, but repels any attempt to map it into the system's interior.**

**Proof 3: Lawvere's Fixed Point Theorem (1969)**

In any Cartesian closed category, if there exists a morphism $f: Y \times A \to A$ such that for every $g: Y \to A$ there exists $y$ satisfying $f(y, a) = g(a)$, and there exists a morphism $\neg: A \to A$ with no fixed point, then for any morphism $\alpha: Y \times A \to A$ there exists $y$ such that $\alpha(y, a) = \neg(\alpha(y, a))$ holds for some $a$. **Lawvere's theorem unifies Russell's paradox, Gödel's incompleteness, Cantor's diagonalization, and Turing's halting problem into the same category-theoretic structure: self-referential fixed points necessarily repel internal inhabitation.**

**Proof 4: Correspondences in Physical Systems**

In thermodynamics, absolute zero ($T = 0$ K) is an uninhabitable fixed point — the Third Law of Thermodynamics says that absolute zero cannot be reached through finite steps. In relativity, the speed of light $c$ is an uninhabitable fixed point — no object with mass can reach the speed of light. **These are not "technical limitations," but the repulsive property inherent in the mathematical structure of physical laws.**

**Cross-Domain Verification:**

| Domain | Uninhabitable Fixed Points |
|------|-----------------|
| Mathematics | Russell's paradox, Cantor's diagonal set, all self-referential paradoxes |
| Computer Science | Undecidability of the halting problem, impossibility of a complete debugger |
| Physics | Absolute zero, speed of light, quantum no-cloning theorem |
| Economics | Perfect competition equilibrium — theoretically exists, but no actual market can "inhabit" it |
| Cybernetics | A perfect self-regulating system — the more precise the regulator, the farther it is from the "center" of the regulated system |

**Universal Assertion:** The Law of Uninhabitability is the philosophical expression of Lawvere's Fixed Point Theorem. In any sufficiently rich category (logical, computational, physical), self-referential closed structures act as a **repellor** — it is not that they do not exist, but that no finite entity can "become" them. This is a theorem of category theory, not a philosophical assumption. It applies to any system with self-referential capacity in any possible world.

### 17.4 Objective Proof of the Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity

**Theorem Statement (Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity):** Higher-level symbols and lower-level physical states are not the same kind of thing. There is no isomorphic mapping between the two, only approximation.

**Proof 1: Non-Isomorphism of Gödel Numbering**

Gödel numbering maps each logical formula to a natural number. But the arithmetic properties of natural number $n$ (such as "$n$ is prime") and the logical properties of formula $F$ (such as "$F$ is provable") are not isomorphic. **Truth in number theory is not equivalent to provability in logic — this is the core of Gödel's incompleteness theorem: number-theoretic truth does not equal logical provability.** If symbols and states were isomorphic, then Gödel numbering would be an isomorphic mapping, but Gödel's theorem precisely proves that it cannot be an isomorphism.

**Proof 2: Shannon's Source-Channel Separation Theorem**

Shannon proved: source coding (compressing information into symbols) and channel coding (mapping symbols to physical signals) are separate, independent optimization problems. **The optimal encoding of symbols and the optimal encoding of physical channels use different mathematical frameworks** — the former focuses on entropy, the latter on mutual information. If symbols and states were isomorphic, the two would be the same problem.

**Proof 3: Hierarchical Nature of Computation Theory**

The Church-Turing thesis states: all computable functions can be computed by a Turing machine. But the physical realization of a Turing machine (silicon chips, neurons, abacus) and its mathematical description (state transition function) exhibit multiple realizability. **The same mathematical Turing machine can be realized in countless physical systems, and the same physical system can simulate countless different Turing machines.** The mapping is many-to-many, not isomorphic.

**Proof 4: Corollary of the Quantum No-Cloning Theorem**

The quantum no-cloning theorem states: an arbitrary unknown quantum state cannot be precisely copied. But classical information (symbols) can be copied infinitely. **If symbols and states were isomorphic, then quantum states (physical states) and classical bits (symbols) would have the same copyability — but they clearly do not.**

**Cross-Domain Verification:**

| Domain | Manifestation of Symbol-State Non-Identity |
|------|---------------------|
| Computer Science | A program's source code (symbols) and its compiled machine code (physical state) are not the same thing |
| Neuroscience | Neuronal firing patterns (physical state) ≠ thought content (symbols) — this is the information-theoretic formulation of the mind-body problem |
| Linguistics | The physical sound waves of speech (state) ≠ the semantics of words (symbols) — the same word can have different physical pronunciations |
| Biology | Genotype (DNA sequence, symbols) ≠ Phenotype (protein structure, physical state) — the genotype-to-phenotype mapping is many-to-many and environment-dependent |
| Physics | The mathematical expression of physical laws (symbols) ≠ the actual operation of the physical world (state) — mathematics is a model, not the world itself |

**Universal Assertion:** The Law of Symbol-State Non-Identity is the formalization of the principle that **the map is not the territory** (Korzybski). In any possible world, as long as there is a distinction between representation and the object represented, the two are necessarily non-isomorphic — because if they were isomorphic, representation would become a kind of copying, and copying itself would require representation, leading to infinite regress. **The gap between representation and the represented is a logical necessity, not a technical limitation.**

### 17.5 Objective Proof of the Law of Primacy of Generative Force

**Theorem Statement (Law of Primacy of Generative Force):** Before all definitions and descriptions, there must exist a primordial force capable of producing distinctions, boundaries, and motion. This force cannot be reduced to the things it produces.

**Proof 1: Axiomatic Necessity of Formal Systems**

Any formal system $T$ requires axioms — a set of initial propositions that are unprovable within $T$. If $T$ attempts to prove its own axioms, it requires the axioms of a stronger system $T'$, leading to infinite regress. **The question "where do axioms come from?" cannot be answered within the formal system — the choice and positing of axioms is itself an irreducible act of generative force.** This is not a defect of mathematics, but determined by the logical structure of formal systems.

**Proof 2: The Failure of Hilbert's Program and Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem**

Hilbert hoped to prove the consistency of mathematics using finitary methods. Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem proved: **any consistent formal system containing Peano arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency.** A consistency proof requires a meta-system stronger than the system itself. **The question "why is the system consistent?" cannot be answered by the system itself — it requires an external, stronger generative force to provide the guarantee.**

**Proof 3: The Unexplained Terms of Physics**

Any physical theory contains unexplained initial conditions or fundamental constants. The Standard Model has 19 free parameters, whose values cannot be derived from within the theory — they are "set." String theory and loop quantum gravity attempt to reduce these parameters, but any theory will ultimately have some unexplained primitive inputs. **Physical laws themselves cannot explain why the laws are the way they are — this requires an external generative force.**

**Proof 4: The Infinite Regress of Causal Chains**

If every event has a cause, then the causal chain either extends infinitely or terminates at a First Cause. Infinite extension results in no true "beginning" (this is not itself contradictory, but evades the question), while termination at a First Cause means: **there exists a "starting point" that can no longer be causally explained — this starting point is the "primordial force" spoken of by the Law of Primacy of Generative Force.**

**Cross-Domain Verification:**

| Domain | Manifestation of Primacy of Generative Force |
|------|-----------------|
| Mathematics | Axioms cannot be proven — they are "posited" |
| Physics | Fundamental constants and initial conditions cannot be derived from theory — they are "measured" |
| Computer Science | Any program requires a "bootloader" not contained within the program itself |
| Biology | The "primordial soup" problem of the origin of life — where did the first self-replicating molecule come from? |
| Logic | Any reasoning requires premises that are not themselves reasoned — Aristotle already pointed this out |

**Universal Assertion:** The Law of Primacy of Generative Force is the statement of the principle that **infinite regress must logically terminate at an irreducible starting point**. In any possible world, as long as the concepts of derivation, causality, and explanation exist, there necessarily exists a limit — a "first mover" that can no longer be derived, no longer be causally explained, no longer be reduced. This is not because "we don't know," but because the concept of "knowing" itself has a hierarchical structure, and hierarchical structure necessarily has a root.

### 17.7 Universality Argument: Why These Laws Apply to Any Possible World

A natural question is: do these laws only apply to "this universe of ours"? If the physical constants were different, or the logical rules were different, would these laws still hold?

**Argument 1: Logical Necessity**

The core proofs of the five foundational laws depend on the following premises:
- Set theory (ZFC or equivalent systems)
- First-order logic
- Peano arithmetic (or equivalent computational theory)

These premises do not depend on any specific physical constants. If there exists a possible world in which set theory, first-order logic, and computational theory still hold, then the five foundational laws hold in that world. **Denying these laws is equivalent to denying mathematics — and mathematics is the prerequisite for the existence of the concept of "information" in any possible world.**

**Argument 2: Physical Independence of Shannon Information Theory**

Shannon's definition of information entropy $H = -\sum p_i \log p_i$ does not depend on any physical constants. It depends only on the existence of probability distributions and the mathematical properties of probability. In any possible world, as long as the concepts of "probability" and "uncertainty" exist, information theory holds, and the Law of Distinction holds.

**Argument 3: Universality of Gödel's Theorem**

The proof of Gödel's incompleteness theorem depends only on the formalization of arithmetic — it does not depend on any physical assumptions. If, in a possible world, natural numbers exist and Peano arithmetic holds, then Gödel's theorem holds, and the Law of Boundary holds. **If natural numbers do not exist, then the concept of "information" itself can hardly exist — because the smallest unit of information (the bit) is itself an instance of the natural numbers 0 and 1.**

**Argument 4: Category-Theoretic Universality of Lawvere's Fixed Point Theorem**

Lawvere's theorem holds in any Cartesian closed category. This means: as long as the "mappings" and "products" in a possible world have the structure of a Cartesian closed category, the Law of Uninhabitability holds in that world. **The structure of a Cartesian closed category is the minimum requirement for the concepts of "function" and "product" — if a possible world does not even have these two concepts, then it cannot have "information."**

**Argument 5: Refutation of "Possible World Exceptionalism"**

Suppose there exists a possible world in which the information laws do not hold. This means:
- Either distinction does not exist (but then "information" cannot be defined);
- Or self-reference does not produce boundaries (but this means Gödel's theorem does not hold, i.e., arithmetic is inconsistent);
- Or self-referential fixed points can be inhabited (but this means Russell's paradox does not produce contradiction, i.e., set theory is inconsistent).

**These "exceptional worlds" are logically equivalent to admitting contradiction — and the principle of explosion (ex contradictione quodlibet) means: in an inconsistent world, any proposition is simultaneously true and false, including "the information laws hold" itself.** Therefore, the information laws hold in all consistent possible worlds.
